# Present SoundProof in five minutes

**Demo:** [Open SoundProof](https://soundproof-music-studio.onrender.com/)  
**Code:** [soundproof-music-studio](https://github.com/gurkiratsingh-gp/soundproof-music-studio)

## Prepare before class

Open the public HTTPS site on the presentation computer and phone. Use headphones on the computer and keep the phone page visible with its screen unlocked. In **Sing it yourself**, pair the phone, choose **Phone microphone**, and press **Check phone microphone**. Speak and confirm the computer’s input meter moves. Arm the controls, save a five-second voice-only take from the phone, and play it back on the computer. Repeat once. Test on the actual classroom network, because a connection that works at home may need a TURN relay on a different network.

Keep one prepared song, one downloaded vocal take, one instrumental WAV, and a short screen recording ready. Check live Kavi responses beforehand if you plan to show them. The free instrumental/recording demonstration does not require a Gemini key. Put the public app URL and repository link on your final slide; keep the phone-pairing invitation private.

## Explain the engineering in your own words

- **React and TypeScript:** the composer, recorder, player, and mobile interface share typed song and recording state.
- **Web Audio and MediaRecorder:** the browser synthesizes instrumentals, mixes tracks, captures a vocal take, and exports audio. Procedural instrumentals are not recordings generated by a trained music model.
- **WebRTC:** a one-time QR/code invitation pairs devices. Express relays connection messages; encrypted microphone audio flows between browsers or through a configured TURN relay. The application server does not store these vocal recordings.
- **Express and authentication:** server-side sessions protect API calls, pairing invitations expire, and provider keys stay on the server.
- **Kavi:** Gemini supplies optional text generation through structured prompts and validated responses. You integrated and constrained a model; you did not train its weights. Support for a language does not guarantee perfect lyrics in it.
- **Storage and tests:** songs and takes stay in the browser, with automated checks for audio behavior, recording lifecycle, and API rules. Explain what was actually tested, and distinguish simulated audio tests from physical-phone testing.

## Be clear about current limits

This is a college/portfolio demo. Accounts currently live in a server file, and Render’s free instance can lose that file when it sleeps, restarts, or redeploys. Browser libraries do not sync across devices; a recreated account cannot automatically reopen an old account’s locally stored library. Download work you want to keep. [Render free-instance limits](https://render.com/docs/free)

Live Kavi output depends on provider configuration and quota. Karaoke center-vocal reduction is an approximation, not guaranteed studio-quality stem separation. AI singing is not offered. The next production steps are durable accounts/database storage, opt-in cloud backups, and reliable TURN service for restrictive networks.

For internship interviews, be ready to explain one reproduced bug, the test used to catch it, and a tradeoff you made.
